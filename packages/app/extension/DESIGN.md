# baited Extension panel

Scope: viewer and broadcaster configuration surfaces, Operate mode. This extends the landing's approved card, Cut Open with the Glass finish and the Prism back, into the actual Twitch panel. Streamers and viewers often keep Twitch in a dark window beside the broadcast, so a dark ground lets the live video retain attention.

The canonical private repository is Komediruzecki/baited. The landing reference is packages/landing, served at baited.maff.app and dev.baited.maff.app. Extension version 0.0.1 is in Twitch Hosted Test, with the test backend at play-baited.maff.app; this is not a review submission or public Extension release. The owner reported successful rounds with Paolo, live Twitch chat and Moonshine. Simulated previews remain separate from those live reports and do not establish device acceptance.

On 27 October 2026 the owner chose the landing's selected card for every surface: the panel, the overlay, the studio, the viewer room and OBS. The landing then shipped Cut Open / Glass / Prism (`packages/landing/selected-cards`), and the owner asked every plain card to follow it: where the art and text sit, the background and the effects. The latest branding decision is the supplied plain original BAITED wordmark, with no bomb or hook branding.

The viewer reads a single secret word, its off-limits clues, and the clock while following a Twitch stream. One Cut Open card carries all three, laid out as on the landing: the state chip and difficulty pips, the art on a violet Glass field that rises from the card's top edge and ends at an angle under the art, then the word in its item's own ink straight on the charcoal face, the clues on the ruled “Keep out of chat” rail, the card's example question, and the BAITED footer whose rule is the round's timer. The art resolves from pixels the first time a round shows it, as on the landing. The streamer's card and the card between rounds show the Prism back in the art window. The footer says “Nudge the conversation.” Waiting cards say “Only chat will know.”

- `card.css` draws the face and the back for the panel, the overlay and the app. Every length is the landing's own pixel value times `--cut-u`, one landing pixel at the card's width, so a 300 px card is the landing's 300 × 420 card and smaller cards keep 9 to 12 px type floors. `tests/card-parity.test.ts` compares its values, the Glass rules, the pixel reveal timing and the Prism image with the landing. Hosts change only the `--cut-*` variables, so the field always meets the card's top edge. One product guard sits on top of the landing values: a streamer's own clue that is longer than any deck clue wraps inside the card instead of running past its edge. Deck clues render as on the landing. A word without art keeps the field at the landing's 80 px minimum.
- Ground `#121016`, Glass field `#9146ff` to `#120d1a` with a lilac rule under the art, card face `#242426`, chips `#1a1522`, card edge `#515057`, copy `#fffdf7` / `#dedce3`, card secondary `#c9c0d1`, panel secondary `#b5afbf`. The word's ink comes from the review catalogue and travels only with the target.
- Violet `#9146FF`, readable violet text `#c9a8ff`, pale yellow `#FFFC79`. The main studio link uses `#7330cf` so normal-size white button text has sufficient contrast.
- Inter for controls, explanations and timing. Russo One for the word. Use the plain original BAITED artwork for branding rather than the older provisional text or hook variants.
- Earlier bomb masters, display derivatives and hook variants remain preserved references. They are not the source of authority for the new product branding.
- All 78 current playable targets have approved illustrations. Clue restrictions are interface text, separate from the artwork, and remain readable without strike-throughs.
- Static defaults; reconnect and authorization states are explicit. No automatic sound, ongoing mascot motion or hidden content that depends on animation. The pixel reveal is the only card motion on the panel: 400 ms, once per round, skipped for reduced motion, a hidden page or an offscreen card. The canvas is emptied whenever the art is cleared, so it never keeps an earlier word's picture. The approved card has no glint.
- Responsive within Twitch's 318 px content width, with a 240 px defensive floor. At 318 × 500, the word, rule, four hard-mode clues, clock and skip action must remain visible. The panel card grows with its copy instead of keeping 5:7, so its art window is 148 landing pixels instead of 176. On 28 October all 78 targets with their hard-mode clues and questions fit at 318 and 240 px in the local harness, and every fixture fitted at 318, 279, 240, 200 and 159 px. While the reconnect notice shows, the art window drops to 108 and the example question steps aside. Below 280 px the chips may take two rows. Time uses tabular numerals. The local harness defaults to the actual 318 × 500 viewport and offers a hard-mode fixture.
- Configuration is a handoff sheet: channel status, one studio action, then numbered microphone/OBS preparation. The first step changes to “Twitch channel connected” after authorization. It has no duplicate game controls. The setup action is still hidden until authorization gives a validated destination.
- Error and reconnect messages remain above the card. Broadcaster and renewed-authorization states keep secrets cleared. No redesign changes request handling, game state, retry behavior or announcements.
- After a confirmed catch the card names the streamer's pick (“Streamer’s pick: @name”). Between rounds it lists chat's top three bait MVPs with their pick counts. During a round both give way to the word, clues and clock. Only names travel, never a hint, and a renewed authorization clears them with everything else. The MVP heading is the card's ruled label, as over the clues, and the signature footer stays; when the pick and the board show together the art window drops to 104 landing pixels to keep the card inside 500 px. Both fields are optional in data version 0.0.1, so an older backend shows neither.

## Video overlay

`overlay.html` is the Video - Fullscreen view. It deals the landing's card onto the live video, at the right edge and vertically centred, clear of the title and player controls. The card is 5:7 and sized from the player height (54 vh, 150–420 px). At full size it is the landing card at its own 300 × 420 px; smaller cards scale with container units and never drop type below 9–12 px. Cards under 340 px tall drop the example question, and under 270 px the rail label, so the word, clues and clock stay legible in the default player. Under 230 px tall the clues take one line each, because two columns of deck clues stop fitting there. Under 160 px the face's chip row steps aside (the back keeps the streak), words step down to 11 px, or 9.5 px past ten letters, instead of splitting, and a word without art gives its field to the copy. On 28 October all 78 targets fitted every player size from 480 × 270 to 1920 × 1080 in the local harness; only TRANSMUTATION wraps, on the two smallest players measured (480 × 270 and 560 × 315). A mini player shows nothing.

- The card lands face down on its Prism back. A viewer's card turns over to the Cut Open face, and its art resolves from pixels as the face comes round; the broadcaster's never does, because the broadcaster's projection carries no target, ink or question. The back shows the clock and chat's streak. A decided round turns over for everyone and leaves after 12 seconds. Between rounds the frame is fully transparent.
- A tab beside the card hides it; the choice is remembered per viewer when storage is available.
- A won card names the streamer's pick and lists the top three bait MVPs; cards under 340 px tall drop the MVP list. A pick made after the card has left brings it back for 12 seconds. A pick that is taken back does not.
- Chat's win streak is public and shown as fire. From two wins in a row, a flame chip sits on both faces. A win shows the last positions as fire coins (the newest ringed, the next ones dim); a win that lands on a badge tier swaps the art for that tier's medallion and names the next badge. The panel adds the streak to its header and result.
- The frame keeps the default color scheme. Declaring `dark` would make browsers paint an opaque canvas behind the transparent overlay.

The Extension ZIP must exclude preview fixtures and test code. Real Twitch authorization is required by the shipped entry points. Local preview state is labelled by its separate harness, not passed off as a live session.
