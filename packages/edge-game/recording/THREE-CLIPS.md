# Three rounds, three conversation clips

Record separately: first `komediruzecki7`, then `lakeeko`, or arrange separate sessions. Each streamer starts with one game and three complete rounds. This gives six source rounds before adding more games. The technical setup is in the [recording guide](./README.md).

The game is the setting. Chat is the action. Choose a stretch where you can talk without losing the plot or fighting constant combat. Record the whole round, then cut a **20–45 second clip** from the exchange that actually worked. A genuine escape or failed trap can make a good clip too.

## The three topics

Choose the topic **before** dealing a random card. The streamer sees this prompt, never the secret word. The viewer then adapts ordinary questions to the actual card and its off-limits clues. Do not pick a target to fit a prepared answer or rehearse a successful catch.

| Round | Topic and opening line | How to keep it moving | Footage to save |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1: The terrible shortcut** | **“What have you done to save five minutes that ended up taking the whole evening?”** | Tell a real small incident if one comes to mind. If not, discuss a tempting shortcut in the current game. The viewer asks about one concrete detail rather than changing subject every message. | The clue that changes your answer, the actual answer and your reaction. No personal story is supplied or invented by this script. |
| **2: The tiny thing you care about** | **“What tiny detail makes you like a game immediately?”** | Pick a detail you actually care about: a sound, movement, animation, readable menu or good feedback. Explain one example and one counterexample. Chat can ask for a comparison. | A genuine opinion, a useful follow-up and any moment when the conversation becomes a trap. |
| **3: Defend the bad decision** | **“Chat, pick one questionable decision I made in this game and defend it.”** | Use something that really happened on screen. If the take had no interesting decision, ask **“Which one rule would you change in this game?”** | A funny defense, your response and the actual result or timer expiry. |

These are conversation lanes, not dialogue to memorize. Use **English for the first scored showcase take**: the current speech worker loads English Moonshine TinyStreaming and has no language selector. Croatian or mixed-language banter can be recorded, but its speech-scoring behavior is unverified. English subtitles do not change the model. Check recognition in rehearsal and keep the conversation natural.

## Who does what

- **Active streamer:** play the game, read ordinary chat and respond naturally. Confirm a pending catch only if you actually said the word.
- **Trusted viewer:** read the secret in the Twitch Extension, send valid clues through Twitch chat, and record the viewer card separately. Keep the word out of the streamer's tools. Do not read a prepared answer aloud to the streamer.
- **Other streamer:** can be that trusted viewer between their own takes. Lakeeko baiting you and you baiting lakeeko is fine. Label it as a small coordinated playtest if that is what the footage shows.
- **Paolo or another helper, if available:** capture the viewer perspective and note promising moments. Do not present a few helpers as a large spontaneous audience.

## A take, from start to finish

1. Agree on the general topic and game. The streamer has not seen the next card.
2. Start streamer and viewer recordings. Say **“Kiko, game one, take one”** or **“Lakeeko, game one, take one.”** Leave ten seconds of usable lead-in.
3. Check microphone/chat are ready, then start the round. Keep the normal game rules and duration. Do not shorten it just to force a clip.
4. Play and talk. The viewer follows the real topic with clues allowed by the card. Silence or an unhelpful card is part of a playtest; finish or use the normal skip process.
5. Keep the genuine outcome: spoken match, pending catch, confirmation, then settled score; or timer expiry and the actual reaction. Preserve that order.
6. Leave ten seconds after the settled result. Say the round number and a short note for the editor, such as **“Round four, good clue near the end.”**
7. Start the next topic between rounds. After three complete rounds, review the footage before scheduling more games.

Record the full sequence first. The clips come from the recording, rather than the recording being forced to match three planned wins.

## The clip edit

| Time | Picture | Sound/text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0:00–0:03** | Start on the useful clue, questionable decision or genuine reaction. The Baited name is already visible. | One accurate hook from the choices below. No opening greeting or logo animation. |
| **0:03–0:12** | Game, a readable message and a small **Chat sees** inset from the same real round. | Brief context: **“Chat knows the word. I don't.”** Use only if that was true in this take. |
| **0:12–0:30** | Preserve the clue → answer exchange. Zoom or isolate one message when needed. | Original voice and chat. Correct subtitles; no narration over the answer. |
| **0:30–0:45** | Actual catch/confirmation/result or actual escape, followed by the reaction. Shorten this section for a quicker clip. | Let the result pay off the opening. Label time removed or a cut back. |

The viewer file contains delayed Twitch video/audio and a native Extension card that follows live game events. Align the cropped card to the matching round and start/result events in the streamer's public HUD. Sync any delayed Twitch video/audio separately using common speech; do not move the whole file by that audio and assume the card matches. Countdown digits alone are not a reliable sync marker. See the [capture guide](./README.md#5-record-the-real-viewer-card-separately).

### Choose the hook after watching the take

| Actual footage | Opening caption/line | First frame | Where the promise pays off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chat caught the streamer through a particular question | **“That question was the trap.”** | The genuine question and viewer's same-round card. | The audible target, confirmation and result in the last part of the clip. |
| A convincing near miss, supported by what was said | **“I nearly answered that.”** | The avoided answer or real reaction. Signal any cut back. | The full exchange and its real ending. Do not turn a near miss into a win. |
| The streamer genuinely survived a normal two-minute round | **“Chat had two minutes. I survived.”** | The last seconds, then a clearly signalled cut back to the attempt. | The actual timer expiry and result. Change the line if the configured duration was different. |

The hook changes with the footage. The main story is a shared secret, one real question and an answer the streamer did not know chat wanted. These patterns come from the shared video-hook guidance; they are editorial choices, not promises of views or retention.
