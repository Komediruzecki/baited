# baited landing

The static baited landing for `baited.maff.app`, with development at `dev.baited.maff.app`. The page runs in three parts, each about one screen tall: a hero whose 32-second staged film flies out into a player in the middle of the screen as you scroll, “How it plays” (four step cards that build a stack as you scroll, then a fan of ten example cards with a small key to the three card levels), and a charcoal footer that closes the page. The card footer joins the light violet “bait” with “them.”. The page footer keeps the Irchiinnuss maker credit, Terms, Privacy and the contact address.

The hero starts muted. Turning sound on is an explicit choice that is not remembered after leaving or reloading. Pause and sound are two small buttons in the film's corner: they show on hover, keyboard focus or a tap, and stay while the film is paused; a click on the picture also pauses it. “Read the round” in the note under the film opens the transcript. The headline reads “One secret word. Your whole chat against you.”, with “secret” hidden under a 13 × 3 pixel mosaic (the word stays in the page's text). The film starts beside it as a tilted 16:9 card. Scrolling straightens the card, squares its corners and grows it into a 16:9 player in the middle of the screen while the headline's lines slide off to the left (`site/showreel.js`, after 1minus1.com). Phones play the same 16:9 film as desktops, with the chat on the right; there the card starts behind the headline and comes forward over the letters as it flies. On desktops at least 1024 px wide, violet ribbons of square pixels cross the hero (`site/hero-scribble.js`, after 1minus1.com's header): one with the page's entrance, then one route after another every few seconds while the hero is on screen. Reduced motion, or no GSAP, keeps the film in the hero. Reduced-motion visitors get chapter stills and can choose to play. The deck supports dragging and flicking, a click on a side card and the arrow keys.

This package does not deploy the game or Extension backend. It has no Twitch sign-in, microphone capture, speech recognition, analytics scripts or game credentials. The separate `/terms` and `/privacy` pages describe the stated test scope; their copy distinguishes the landing from the personal Extension test.

## Motion

Motion uses GSAP 3.15.0 with ScrollTrigger, self-hosted in `site/vendor/gsap/` so the self-only content policy still applies. Both files load with `defer` before the page modules. Without GSAP, the steps show as a plain list, the deck changes cards without animation and headings appear at once.

The page has no yellow: at the owner's request the palette is the brand violets, white and ink. The only warm colors left are the cards' own item colors (BANANA is yellow, VOLCANO orange) and the speech check, which marks VOLCANO in its card's orange. The hero film was rendered again with VOLCANO in the same orange (see ASSETS.md).

The header is the navigation (`site/nav.js`), after monochrome-studio.com's header and the owner's screen recording of it: a dark pill centred at the top with the pixel BAITED logo (`site/pixel-logo.js`) in its own cell, “How to play”, “Cards” and a violet chat button. Hovering a link breaks its word into pixels that fly into a pixel icon (a gamepad, two cards) as a violet square fades in under them; hovering the logo sends a wave through its columns while the cell fills with lavender pixels. The chat button's bubble sits in the middle of its square and keeps typing. While the visitor scrolls down the links fold away and the chat button widens with its words, “Get in touch”; scrolling up or keyboard focus opens the bar again. A phone always shows this compact bar. The bar slides away while the footer fills the top of the screen, since the footer has its own buttons. Without JavaScript it simply stays open.

The pixel layer, also after monochrome-studio.com, uses square pixels throughout:

- `site/pixel-boot.js` (a small blocking script in `<head>`) covers the first paint, and `site/pixels.js` swaps the cover for violet squares that disappear one by one in random order. A link to another page of the site (Terms, Privacy, back home) first fills the screen with squares, then loads the page. Hash links, new tabs, mail and other sites are left alone. If the script never runs, the cover fades by itself after 2.5 seconds. Reduced motion turns the curtain off. It does not need GSAP.
- `site/pixels.css` draws every icon as pixel art on a small grid (arrows, chat bubble, play, pause, sound, microphone), and its pixel buttons put the icon in an ink box that spreads over the whole button on hover while the icon loops: arrows hop a pixel, the bubble's dots bounce like someone typing. The navigation's chat button keeps typing all the time.
- `site/reveal.js` uncovers text as if a highlighter lifted off it: each line of a section title, the How it plays lead, the cards' intro and the deck's help line starts under a violet bar, and the bars draw back one line after another as the text comes into view. The text gets its original markup back when it is shown. The hero and the footer title have no reveal.
- A foil glint sweeps over a card when it comes to the front of the deck, and over the VOLCANO card once it is dealt.
- Four lilac viewfinder corners hold the hero film, a small idea from fortnite.com. They close in once when the page opens and a little more on hover.
- Leaving the tab changes its title to “🤐 Don’t say it…” until the visitor comes back.

`site/why-stack.js` builds “How it plays”, adapted from the voicetaggods.com why-stack. On desktop with a mouse the stack stays pinned beside the intro, centred on the screen. The first card is already on the stack when the section arrives; each further card rises from the bottom of the screen, tilted ±10°, onto the stack as the visitor scrolls (one scrubbed timeline, one card per scroll step). On touch screens and phones the cards stack with `position: sticky` instead.

`site/why-scenes.js` animates the picture inside each step card once the card arrives: the real VOLCANO card is dealt and flips face up; Twitch chat keeps receiving hints in a seamless loop; the streamer's screen shows the island game with a talking facecam, a smoking crater and the round clock, while the studio bar keeps the word hidden; and the speech check boxes VOLCANO and tags it “Detected”. Loops only run for the card in front while the section is on screen. The chat, names and lines come from the staged film.

The step cards use Material Design 3 color roles generated from the brand violet `#9146FF` with Google's material-color-utilities (SchemeContent): primary, secondary and tertiary containers and a surface container, each with its matching “on” color for text. The screens inside the cards use the same scheme's dark surfaces. The values and their roles are listed at the top of `site/why-stack.css`.

`site/deck.js` drives the fan. Its geometry and timing were measured from the owner's flick-cards reference video: neighbours at 9.8°, outer cards at 15°, both dimmed; a drag of about 1.87 card widths moves the fan by one card; a release flicks exactly one card (or keeps the nearest card past halfway) with a spring that overshoots about 17% and settles in 0.6 s, while the arriving card lifts briefly. Only the front card shows its word and clues. On a phone the fan closes up to fit the screen: the cards two away from the front stay 10 px inside its edges (`fanSpread`).

Every card face shows its level as three simple dots and the secret word centred in its item's own color. The approved landing design is **Cut Open / Glass / Prism**, with Ruled clues under “Keep out of chat.” Its purple ruled surface ends at an angle above the title. There is no inner rectangle around the illustration, and the complete sprite fits inside a four-pixel inset. Each gallery card's art first shows as coarse pixels of its own colours and comes clear when the card first settles; reduced motion shows the finished image immediately. With a mouse, the art leaves a trail of pixel blocks behind the pointer that clear one by one, after 1minus1.com. Alternative editions, surfaces and clue treatments remain in the development Card Lab.

Both landing environments include the approved Ribbon Spread: pull more than roughly half the visible deck width slowly for at least 260 ms, or choose “spread the deck.” The cards turn face up and stand on the inside of a wide curve, so the ones toward the ends turn to face the middle. The row runs to the edges of its sheet and is cut off hard, without a fade: on the left where the sheet stops short of the screen, and on the right at the screen's edge. At rest an end card stands across each cut. A short or fast flick keeps the original fan behavior. The open row never moves by itself: drag to scrub (a throw coasts and settles on the nearest card), or use arrows or click a side card to stop at it. A passing mouse pushes a card along and it springs back, and a fast-moving row splits its art into red and cyan (`ribbon/ribbon-effects.js`). Escape, “Back to the deck” or clicking the center card gathers the nearest card. Small vertical scrolls and mouse movement outside the row keep it open. Scrolling gathers it once less than half the central card remains visible, including coverage by the rising footer. Native touch cancellation pauses the row and leaves page scrolling available. Reduced motion opens a still row; hidden tabs gather it.

The public tasting deck contains ten fixed examples, chosen in `scripts/public-samples.mjs`. Builds serialize only those records and include only their source artwork and display-sized WebP illustrations under `/assets/card-deck/`. The full authored source catalogue and all 78 game cards remain intact; the development-only Card Lab still compares them. That public review URL and previously published versions mean the word bank is not confidential. The live target remains protected by the game's role projections. Both landing builds use the same presentation modules and Prism back, independent of the Lab. Artwork references and pixel buffers remain bounded during repeated browsing.

The hero, the “How it plays” stage and “The cards” each fill one screen (`100svh`) with their content centred. On short desktop screens the deck scales down to 88%, 78% or 68% so the fan and its level key still fit; the deck measures that scale, so a drag still moves one card per 1.87 card widths.

`site/footer.js` closes the page. The footer takes the cards' charcoal face (`#242426`), with their off-white `#fffdf7` for its letters (14.5:1) and the brand violet `#7721e5` for the giant logo and the button's hover. It rises over “The cards” as soon as the deck has arrived, with no hold before it, while the deck stays pinned beneath it, and its top edge arrives as a band of charcoal square pixels, after the owner's pixel-transition reference: four rows of about 90 px squares that fill nearest rows first, in a random order that is the same on every visit. With a mouse the circle and the film button lean toward the pointer. The layout follows the owner's references. From dennissnellenberg.com it takes the two-line title with a round picture and one rule with a violet “Get in touch” circle on it; hovering the circle fills it with ink and a typing pixel bubble. From monochrome-studio.com it takes the bar under the rule: version and maker on the left and the pages with small pixel icons in the middle of the page. “Watch the round” sits centred in the free space between that bar and the giant logo, with as much room above it as below. From flowty.co it takes the giant violet BAITED logo running off the bottom edge. The logo is stretched a little past the screen so the visible part of its letters touches both sides exactly, with the same space on the left and the right. It assembles from charcoal squares as the visitor reaches the end.

Reduced motion shows the steps as a list with every scene in its final state, and changes deck cards instantly. The footer then follows the deck without pixels or pinning, and the curtain, text reveals, hero ribbons, glints and icon loops are off. The hero film and approved card artwork are unchanged.

## Checks

From the baited workspace root:

```sh
rtk pnpm --filter @baited/landing check
rtk pnpm --filter @baited/landing test:run
rtk pnpm --filter @baited/landing deploy:dry-run
rtk pnpm --filter @baited/landing deploy:dry-run:prod
```

The deck tests cover the fan's slot geometry, wraparound, the release rules, the spring and lift curves and how the fan closes up on narrow screens. The scene tests cover the round clock, the chat and facecam timing, and that the chat never types the secret word. The footer tests cover the pixel edge's columns, fill order, fade and fixed pattern, and the logo's size and squares. The card tests check every secret word's color against the card face and that the first step card uses the deck's face. The pixel tests cover the curtain's size and which links get it, the navigation's float, fold and step-away rules, the film's flight and the phone mask that brings it over the headline, the hero ribbons' routes and square sweep, the sheets' deal, the highlighter bars, the drawn scrollbar, that every page loads the curtain early and that no served file uses the old yellow. The package's Worker tests cover host and environment guards, read-only serving, indexing headers, robots, sitemap, unpublished proof routes and response preservation. Brand tests restrict proof selection to known variants on review hosts. Browser verification must also cover the film, the scroll-built steps, deck dragging and legal navigation with the actual Worker content policy.

`pnpm --filter @baited/landing test:browser` runs the responsive regression checks in headless Chromium against a local Worker. Install Chromium first with `pnpm --filter @baited/landing exec playwright install chromium`. Set `BAITED_TEST_OUTPUT` to a registered run's `scratch/raw-logs/` directory for screenshots and failure traces; CI uses its runner temporary directory. Set `BAITED_TEST_URL` to check an already running preview or deployed host instead. The tests cover mobile button alignment, the intro-to-card gap, the footer wipe starting as soon as the deck stops, repeated footer navigation, direct card links and reduced motion. The local test server uses the installed workerd's supported compatibility date; deployment configuration is unchanged.

## Development and production

Landing builds require Linux `flock` from util-linux, available on this workstation and the Ubuntu CI runners. Collaborators on Windows or macOS can run these builds in WSL/Linux. Each environment has its own bounded build lock, so multiple previews cannot delete and rebuild the same output simultaneously. Kernel ownership ends when a builder exits; retained lock files do not need stale-lock deletion.

Compilation happens in a private staging directory. Development publishes complete files atomically and publishes HTML last, so Wrangler can read the current output throughout a rebuild. Unchanged files keep their inode. Previous generated files, including old hashed chunks, remain for tabs and asset snapshots that still reference them. To clear that local history, stop the previews first, remove only `packages/landing/dist/site-dev`, then build again. Production always publishes a fresh staged tree and retains no previous lab assets.

The development build includes a standalone Card Lab at `/card-lab/`. It uses synthetic state without connecting to the game or Twitch. Gallery code, fonts and artwork stay beneath that path. The production build physically omits the gallery and the brand proof lab, and the Worker returns 404 for both prefixes.

Local review on this workstation (the installed local workerd currently supports 2026-08-08):

```sh
rtk proxy timeout --foreground 10800s pnpm --dir /home/maff/foss/worktrees/baited/unified-workspace/packages/landing exec wrangler dev --env dev --local --ip 127.0.0.1 --port 4370 --inspector-port 9370 --compatibility-date 2026-08-08 --log-level warn
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:4370/>. The explicitly assigned port is fixed; the process stops after three hours or Ctrl+C. The local compatibility override is not a change to the deployment configuration. CI validates the configured cloud bundle.

[Deploy landing](../../.github/workflows/deploy-landing.yml) preserves the development and production release rules. It is gated by the repository variable `LANDING_DEPLOY_ENABLED=true`; the gate starts off until Cloudflare secret configuration and deployment ownership have been checked. The old disjoint-colliders deployment workflow must be disabled before this one is enabled, so two repositories cannot overwrite the same Worker.

Once enabled, merging landing changes or their deployment inputs into `main` deploys `maff-bait-dev` to `dev.baited.maff.app` and compares the served HTML with the merged source. A manual run from `main` also targets development. Production requires a separately authorized annotated `bait-vX.Y.Z` tag matching `packages/landing/package.json`, on a commit already in `main`. It deploys `maff-bait` to `baited.maff.app`. Feature branches do not auto-deploy. Never move a published tag.

The former `bait.maff.app` and `dev.bait.maff.app` domains remain attached to the same Workers as redirect aliases. GET and HEAD requests receive a permanent 308 redirect to their matching `baited` hostname, preserving the path and query. Redirect destinations always use fixed HTTPS origins; production rejects other hostnames. The development proof selector recognizes only the new development hostname and local loopback hosts.


For an authorized recovery, the package scripts are `deploy:dev` and `deploy:prod`. Local credentials must come through Proton Pass. No credential values belong in this package.

The Worker accepts GET and HEAD, serves only the configured environments, and applies a self-only content policy. Development remains `noindex, nofollow`; that discourages indexing but is not access control. An unset environment or mismatched production host returns 404. Successful production responses are indexable. This source version alone is not evidence of a deployment or a published tag.

No deployment is implied by these source files. Confirm the exact revision and served pages in the workflow result before reporting a deployment complete.
