## Overview

The bomb's censored-symbol band becomes a strip that opens when the target is spoken. Its squared pixel silhouette, white hook and hot orange body supply the graphic language. The page behaves like a short animated game title sequence you can step through. The subject itself occupies the page; it is not boxed inside a generic website illustration.

The owner fixed the bomb, Twitch-adjacent purple/yellow/charcoal and the ambition for motion. Cartridge Shelf contributes object-based explanation; Holo Cases contributes a card that changes angle and state when selected. Their scenery and branding are not copied.

## Colors

Committed violet fields (#5426ed), near-black print (#17131e), yellow actions and closing field (#fff35b), pale yellow cards (#f9df8c), warm white (#fffdf7), and muted lavender copy (#ded4ff). The supplied orange/yellow bomb remains the warmest object. Purple is a region, not a glow around dark containers. White is reserved for legible copy and the hook.

## Typography

Angular, heavy display lettering echoes the supplied mark. Use the bundled Russo One for short titles, target words and card headings, with Inter for instructions. Inter is body text only; Russo One carries the identity. Do not claim Russo One is the source logo's exact font. Text remains semantic, including large stage words.

## Layout

One full-width stage, one short instruction strip, one tactile card selection area and one setup invitation. Desktop uses overlap and deliberate scale differences. Mobile recomposes in reading order: offer, action, example, controls. The secret word in this scene is explicitly part of an example, never real streamer information.

## Elevation & Depth

Card stock has a physical edge, a short cast shadow and a restrained perspective tilt. Large graphic layers overlap with hard edges. Avoid generic translucent panels, blurry atmospheric orbs and rainbow foil. The bomb keeps its pixel artwork.

## Shapes

Square-cornered print blocks, narrow rule bars, cut card corners and the single curved white hook. The hook is the contrasting organic shape; do not scatter decorative arrows or unrelated circles.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do make the sequence concrete: a hidden BANANA card, a smoothie question, a spoken BANANA, a shared win.
- Do let visitors replay, pause and step through it. Motion cannot be the only way to understand the game.
- Do use one user-triggered 11.5-second progression: card deal, chat prompt, spoken word, censor reveal, shared win. The bomb reacts to the reveal. Pause freezes the timeline, replay resets it, and five manual beat buttons show stable moments. No endless float on every item.
- Do pause when the tab is hidden. Reduced motion removes travel, rotation and scaling while preserving the five readable states. Changing the example word leaves playback paused.
- Do keep audio off by default and respect reduced motion.
- Don't imply a real livestream, native Twitch Extension approval or a working speech provider in the mockup.
- Don't turn the card showcase into three generic feature tiles.
- Don't replace the working landing or move to bait.maff.app in this study.
