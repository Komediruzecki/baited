# Baited product surfaces

## Scope and mode

The standalone streamer studio and setup flow are Operate surfaces. Streamers use them beside Twitch and OBS, often on a dim second monitor. Preserve the approved landing's charcoal card stock, violet, restrained yellow, BAITED wordmark B, Russo One headings and supplied bomb, while making the next action readable at a glance.

## Direction contract

- **Thesis:** a round desk, with the current round and its controls on the left and microphone feedback on the right. Remove the oversized dashboard sidebar and decorative microphone orbit.
- **World:** `#121016` ground, `#242426` card faces, `#9146ff` selected actions, `#fffc79` result emphasis, off-white text. Edges are precise, corners modest, no glowing panels.
- **Story:** connect the channel, check the mic, start the round, judge catches. The word stays hidden except for the explicit demo-only test reveal. A live Twitch room stays hidden even on localhost.
- **First viewport:** compact brand/navigation/account masthead; round status, timer and action board; separate microphone column with optional transcript below it. Scores occupy one flat strip.
- **Form:** extend the user-approved landing identity into functional product controls. Opus's completed critique makes the card the common object: back to the streamer, face to chat, confirmed reveal on OBS. Codex implemented this polish while Opus quota was unavailable.

## Type and controls

Inter carries labels, inputs, navigation and explanatory text. The approved B asset supplies the wordmark; Russo One is reserved for round headings, revealed words and the timer. Buttons use consistent 44px minimum targets, visible focus, clear disabled states and a modest 160ms state transition. Settings use a native dialog and return keyboard focus to the opener when closed. No idle decorative animation or sound autoplay.

## State and privacy rules

- Preserve disconnected, model-loading, microphone-off, armed, listening, paused, settling, review, win and escape states.
- Start/resume readiness and microphone lease rules are unchanged.
- Moonshine transcript stays opt-in, right-hand-side on desktop, bounded in tab memory and available only under the existing local/demo gate.
- The demo target reveal is explicit. The separately opted-in local Moonshine transcript diagnostic remains available when testing Twitch connections.
- Setup exposes viewer and overlay links with their different information boundaries. The OBS source never receives the active target.
- Errors and confirmations must name the action and its consequence. Preserve all existing recovery controls.

## Responsive behavior

The masthead becomes two rows on phones; navigation remains labeled. The round console precedes the card and microphone in document order. At 320 × 640, the timer and start/pause controls remain above the fold. The settings control becomes a labelled icon. Setup rows collapse, privacy actions sit below their text and long URLs remain inside inputs. Word length classes prevent accidental mid-word splits in the current deck. Reduced-motion suppresses card dealing and turning.

## Card behavior

Studio review uses a 190 px charcoal result card beside the confirm/reject decision on desktop. The active secret stays hidden. Native viewers get the word's approved illustration when one exists; generic words reserve no empty art area. Off-limits clues use struck capitals. A native card deals once per new round over 280 ms, with no urgency pulse.

OBS retains its 540 × 137 HUD on a transparent canvas. Only confirmed catches and escapes receive a 300 × 420 result card at x 40, y 200 on a 1920 × 1080 source. It deals in, turns, holds and leaves within 5.5 seconds. Reconnecting does not restart the whole reveal. Pending catches expose neither the word nor its illustration URL. Reduced-motion shows a stationary card for the same bounded interval.

## Validation boundary

Browser screenshots and simulated game states prove layout and controls only. Real Twitch authorization, physical microphone behavior, actual OBS capture and device acceptance need their corresponding live checks. The completed Opus critique informed this implementation; a fresh Opus review and the owner's visual acceptance remain separate from the local checks.
